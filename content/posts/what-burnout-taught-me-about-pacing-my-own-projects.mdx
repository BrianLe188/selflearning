A couple of years back I ran a side project into the ground by treating every week like a sprint. No real days off, no slack in the schedule, and a running mental list of everything still left to do. It worked for a while, right up until it didn't.

Recovering from that took longer than the burnout itself did to arrive, and it changed how I plan everything I build now.

## The rules I actually follow

I cap deep-work sessions instead of chasing one more hour. I plan slack into every week on purpose, not just when things go wrong. And I've learned to notice the specific feeling of forcing myself through work versus genuinely wanting to do it — the first one is my signal to stop, not push harder.

None of this makes the work move faster in the short term. It's what's let me keep doing this for years instead of a few intense months.
