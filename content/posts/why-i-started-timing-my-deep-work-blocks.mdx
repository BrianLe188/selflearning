title: "Why I started timing my deep-work blocks"
date: "2026-04-18"
tag: "Life"
excerpt: "A simple log that changed how I plan my days more than any productivity app did."
---

For a long time I had a rough sense of how much "real" focused work I got done in a day, but it was really just a feeling — optimistic on good days, unreliable on bad ones. I started keeping a plain log: start time, end time, one line on what I worked on. Nothing automated, no app, just a text file.

## What the log actually showed

My honest deep-work total was consistently lower than I assumed, and it clustered almost entirely into two windows I hadn't consciously noticed before. Meetings scattered through the day were costing me far more than their scheduled length, because of how long it took to get back into focus afterward.

## What changed because of it

I started protecting those two windows on purpose instead of letting anything fill them, and I stopped scheduling anything else during them. The log itself became less important once the habit was in place — the value was in what it taught me, not in keeping it forever.
