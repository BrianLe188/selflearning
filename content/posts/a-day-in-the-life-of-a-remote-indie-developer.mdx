People ask what a typical day looks like when you work for yourself, out of a spare room, with no commute and no standup meeting to anchor the morning. The honest answer is: it varies more than I'd like, but a few things repeat often enough to call a pattern.

Mornings are for deep work — the kind of coding that needs an uninterrupted hour or two before the rest of the day's noise creeps in. I keep meetings out of that window on purpose, even when it means saying no to a call someone would rather have earlier.

## The afternoon shift

By early afternoon the mode changes: client calls, code review, the kind of work that benefits from other people being awake and reachable. It's a different kind of tired than the morning's focused work, and I've learned to stop fighting that difference instead of trying to force one long productive block.

## What I'd tell someone starting out

Build the day around your own energy, not around what a "normal" workday is supposed to look like. Nobody's checking, and the work is better for it.
